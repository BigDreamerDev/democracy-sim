# The Constitution of the Republic of McServerLandia

*Adopted by the Citizens. Amendable by the Citizens. Repealable by the Citizens.*

This Constitution is written to be changed. Where it is silent, it means to be silent. Where it is broad, it means to be broad. Nothing in it is settled, and nothing in it is defended against the people who wrote it.

---

## Article 1 — The Republic and its Citizens

1. A **Citizen** is a member of the WhatsApp Group known as MC Server 3, together with any successor to that Group, however named and however constituted.
2. Citizenship begins with membership and ends with it, and is otherwise unconditional.
3. Every Citizen holds, for the whole of their membership, the right to vote, the right to hold public office as this Constitution provides, the right to speak in the affairs of the Republic, and the right to act against injustice in defence of the nation.
4. What constitutes injustice is not defined here. It is left to the conscience of the Citizen and the judgement of the People.
5. No Citizen may be deprived of citizenship by any office of the Republic. Only the Group may add or remove its own members.

## Article 2 — The Sovereignty of the People

1. All authority in the Republic is held by the Citizens, and every office exercises only such authority as the Citizens have lent it.
2. A **Supermajority** is two thirds of all Citizens, voting as one body.
3. A Supermajority may do anything. It may make law, unmake law, create offices, abolish offices, overrule any decision of any officer, amend this Constitution, replace it, or dispense with it altogether.
4. This Constitution places no question beyond the reach of a Supermajority, and shall not be read as placing any question beyond its reach.
5. Where a Supermajority has spoken, no office of the Republic may act against it, and any act so taken is void.

## Article 3 — The House

1. The legislative power is held by the **House**.
2. The House consists of a number of seats equal to one sixth of the Citizens, disregarding any fraction, and never fewer than three — or of such other number as ordinary law may from time to time provide.
3. Members of the House are elected individually by the Citizens.
4. The House exists to serve the Citizens: to make policy and law in defence of the Republic and its members, and to give effect to the will of the people.
5. The House may regulate its own proceedings in any manner it chooses, save as ordinary law otherwise provides.

## Article 4 — The Speaker

1. The House shall have a **Speaker**, chosen from among its own members.
2. A member becomes Speaker on receiving two thirds of the whole House — counted against every seat, filled or not, and not merely against those who troubled to vote. Where two thirds is not a whole number of votes, the next whole number above it is required.
3. This is a threshold and not a contest. The member with the most votes does not become Speaker by having the most votes. Where no member reaches the required number, no one is chosen, the chair stays empty, and the House ballots again.
4. Each failed ballot lowers the requirement by one vote. It falls no further than a simple majority of the House, and stops there.
5. While the chair is empty no bill may be tabled, and the business of the House waits. The House may still sit, debate, amend, and decide anything that does not require tabling.
6. A Supermajority of the Citizens may appoint a Speaker directly, at any time, and may in this way end a deadlock the House cannot end for itself.
7. The Speaker carries into the House the voice and the demands of the people, decides what business the House shall consider and when it shall decide it, and holds such further powers as ordinary law provides.
8. The Speaker may be removed by two thirds of the House, or by a Supermajority of the Citizens.
9. Every fraction and every number in this Article may be changed by ordinary law, including the two thirds, the rate at which it falls, and the floor beneath which it does not.

## Article 5 — The President

1. The executive power is held by a single Citizen, styled the **President**, elected by a majority of the votes of the Citizens.
2. The President advises the House what law ought to be made, approves the law the House has made, gives effect to the demands of the people, and keeps the Constitution in the will of the people.
3. The President may veto any law passed by the House, in the interests of the people.
4. A veto may be set aside by the House, by such fraction as ordinary law provides.
5. The President may act in any matter this Constitution and the law have not assigned elsewhere.

## Article 6 — Elections and the Electoral Cycle

1. An **Electoral Cycle** is a period of seven days. The fifth and sixth days are days of campaign. The seventh is the day of election.
2. Elections to the House and to the Presidency are held on the day of election, unless the House or a Supermajority provides otherwise.
3. Every Citizen has one vote in any election, and that vote is final once cast.
4. Ordinary law shall provide how votes are counted, how ties are settled, how candidates are nominated, and how a poll may be delayed, extended, annulled, or rerun.
5. Where this Constitution does not say how an election is to be run, it may be run in any way the House provides and the people accept.

## Article 7 — Eligibility and Tenure

1. No Citizen may hold more than one seat in public office at one time.
2. No Citizen may hold office in two consecutive Electoral Cycles, unless a Supermajority provides otherwise, either generally or in a particular case.
3. Any Citizen may stand for any office for which they are eligible, and none may be refused a nomination except as ordinary law provides.
4. A Citizen may resign any office at any time, and need give no reason.

## Article 8 — The Making of Law

1. Any Citizen may propose a law, an amendment to a law, or the repeal of a law.
2. A proposal becomes law when it has passed the House and received the assent of the President, or when a vetoed proposal has been carried again as Article 5 provides.
3. Every other question of procedure — how a proposal is supported, tabled, debated, divided upon, what majority carries it, what quorum is required, and what record is kept — is left to ordinary law, and ordinary law may change it at any time.
4. Law made under this Article binds every Citizen and every office, including the office that made it.
5. All laws, policies and acts of the Republic are subject to change evermore, as the House determines or the people decide by referendum.

## Article 9 — Referendum and the Initiative

1. The Citizens may decide any question directly, by referendum.
2. A referendum may be called by the House, by the President, or by any group of Citizens amounting to one third of their number.
3. The result of a referendum stands above any act of the House or the President on the same question.
4. A referendum may repeal a law, direct an officer, settle a disputed reading of this Constitution, or resolve any other matter whatsoever.
5. Nothing prevents the people from being asked the same question twice.

## Article 10 — Removal from Office

1. The House may impeach the President where the President has acted against the will of the people, and on impeachment the office falls vacant.
2. The Citizens may remove any officer of the Republic by a Supermajority, at any time, for any reason or for none.
3. The House may be dissolved and its seats vacated by a Supermajority, or by the President with the consent of a Supermajority, or as ordinary law provides.
4. Removal from office is not a punishment and carries no further consequence unless ordinary law provides one.

## Article 11 — Interpretation

1. Where this Constitution is silent, the silence is permission: the House may act, and the President may act, and neither is restrained by the omission.
2. Where this Constitution is unclear, the House may adopt a reading of it, and that reading stands until the people decide otherwise by referendum.
3. Where two provisions conflict, the later prevails; where their order cannot be determined, the people shall choose between them.
4. No reading of this Constitution is authoritative merely because it is old, or because it was intended by those who wrote it.
5. This Constitution is to be read as a grant of power to the people, never as a restraint upon them.

## Article 12 — Extraordinary Circumstances

1. Where the House and the President jointly declare that the ordinary working of the Republic is not equal to its circumstances, they may exercise such powers as the moment requires, for such time as the declaration specifies.
2. A declaration under this Article suspends only what it names, and only for as long as it says.
3. A declaration may be ended at any moment by a Supermajority, and may be ended by the House alone.
4. Powers taken under this Article do not become ordinary powers by being used.

## Article 13 — The Record

1. Every election, every division, every enactment and every act of every office shall be recorded, and the record shall be open to every Citizen.
2. Nothing in the record may be deleted. It may only be superseded, and what is superseded remains readable.
3. A repealed law remains in the record as a repealed law. A replaced Constitution remains in the record as a replaced Constitution.
4. This Article may be amended like any other, but the record made under it survives its amendment.

## Article 14 — Amendment

1. This Constitution may be amended by a Supermajority.
2. An amendment may add to it, remove from it, rewrite it in whole or in part, or substitute an entirely different instrument in its place.
3. No article is entrenched. No article requires more than a Supermajority. This Article requires no more than a Supermajority, and may itself be amended to require less, or to require nothing at all.
4. An amendment takes effect on its adoption and is entered in the record as a new version, the old version remaining readable.

## Article 15 — Impermanence

1. Nothing established by this Constitution is permanent, and that includes the Republic, the House, the Presidency, the vote, and this Constitution.
2. A Supermajority may abolish any office, any branch, any election and any right described here.
3. A Supermajority may vest the whole authority of the Republic in one Citizen, in a few, or in none, and may make that vesting indefinite.
4. A Supermajority may adopt for the Republic any form of government whatsoever, whether it resembles this one or not, and no provision of this Constitution shall be read to prevent it. This Constitution consents in advance to its own replacement.
5. This Constitution is not defended against the Citizens. It is defended only against everyone else.
6. The right of the Citizens to take back what they have given away survives the giving away of it, and cannot be signed away in advance, however plainly the words attempt it. Where the Republic has ceased to be a democracy, a Supermajority suffices to make it one again.

## Article 16 — Commencement

1. This Constitution comes into force on its adoption and replaces the instrument that preceded it.
2. Everything lawfully done before its adoption remains lawfully done.
3. Officers holding office at its adoption continue in office until the end of the current Electoral Cycle.
